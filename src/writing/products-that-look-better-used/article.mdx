## Designed to age

There was always something interesting in how some products we use every day use only better with time. Normally we'd assume that everything we buy, we buy because of how it looked initially. There are many examples for this, the iPhone that looks extremely well designed on Apple's website, the clean clothes models wear on fashion sites, or simply products we see when walking past them in showcases.

There are some types of products and their materials which - I believe - makes them look only better with time. I want to use this occasion to epxlore these products to find out the _why_ behind this and what makes them look so much better when used.

## Rimowa

This is very likely one of the best known examples: The Rimowa luggage. Latest once you had to check it in for a flight you'll find scratches on it. After some time I realized how much I like this inperfect design - maybe because each detail represents a memory of a past experience.

## Aesop

Aesop does a great job using shots of their products in an _used_ state, rather than showing them perfectly. I think that the pressed tubes look so much better, more natural. Even tho you can clearly see the inperfect version was rendered it still reminds me of myself using this product on a daily basis.

## Skate deck

This here is more on the extreme side. Compared to the products above, I like how skate decks are made to disappear within a short amount of time when actively skating. I like the idea of skateboard artworks turning into something more unique after usage vs. staying mass-distributed images everyone wanders around with. The beauty of it is that after a while you won't even recognize the original artwork anymore, leaving others to use their imagination to see what it once might have been.
